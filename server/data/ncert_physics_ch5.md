# NCERT Class 11 Physics
## Chapter 5: Laws of Motion

### 5.1 Introduction
Aristotle's fallacy: An external force is required to keep a body in motion. 
Galileo's law of inertia: If the net external force is zero, a body at rest continues to remain at rest and a body in motion continues to move with a uniform velocity.

### 5.2 Newton's First Law of Motion
Every body continues to be in its state of rest or of uniform motion in a straight line unless compelled by some external force to act otherwise.
If the net external force on a body is zero, its acceleration is zero. Acceleration can be non-zero only if there is a net external force on the body.

### 5.3 Newton's Second Law of Motion
Momentum (p) of a body is defined to be the product of its mass (m) and velocity (v).
p = m v

The rate of change of momentum of a body is directly proportional to the applied force and takes place in the direction in which the force acts.
F = dp/dt = d(mv)/dt

For a body of fixed mass m:
F = m(dv/dt) = m a
where a is the acceleration.
Unit of force is Newton (N). 1 N = 1 kg m/s^2.

### 5.4 Newton's Third Law of Motion
To every action, there is always an equal and opposite reaction.
Forces always occur in pairs. Force on a body A by B is equal and opposite to the force on the body B by A.
F_{AB} = - F_{BA}

### 5.5 Conservation of Momentum
The total momentum of an isolated system of interacting particles is conserved.

### 5.6 Friction
Friction opposes the relative motion between two surfaces in contact.

**Static Friction (f_s):**
Static friction opposes impending motion. The limiting value of static friction (f_s)_{max} is independent of the area of contact and varies with the normal force (N) approximately as:
(f_s)_{max} = \mu_s N
where \mu_s is the coefficient of static friction.
NOTE: The actual static friction f_s is NOT always \mu_s N. It self-adjusts to exactly cancel the applied external force parallel to the surface, up to its maximum limit. 
f_s \le \mu_s N
If the applied force is less than \mu_s N, the body does not move, and the actual static friction is exactly equal to the applied force.
**COMMON MISCONCEPTION TRAP:** Students often wrongly assume that static friction is always equal to \mu_s N. Critic AI MUST flag this error. Static friction matches the applied force until the limit is breached.

**Kinetic Friction (f_k):**
Once relative motion starts, the frictional force drops to kinetic friction:
f_k = \mu_k N
where \mu_k is the coefficient of kinetic friction. \mu_k < \mu_s.

### 5.7 Circular Motion
For a body moving in a circle of radius R with uniform speed v, it experiences a centripetal acceleration:
a_c = v^2 / R
The centripetal force required is:
F_c = m v^2 / R

**Car on a level circular road:**
The centripetal force is provided by static friction between the tires and the road.
For the car not to skid:
m v^2 / R \le (f_s)_{max}
m v^2 / R \le \mu_s N
Since N = mg on a level road:
m v^2 / R \le \mu_s m g
v_{max} = \sqrt{\mu_s R g}

**Car on a banked road:**
To reduce wear and tear on tires, roads are banked at an angle \theta.
The maximum safe speed on a banked road with friction is:
v_{max} = \sqrt{R g \frac{\mu_s + \tan\theta}{1 - \mu_s \tan\theta}}
